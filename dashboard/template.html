    This Week's Meals
    Breakfast, lunch, and snacks are batch-cooked once and repeat all week — that's the point, less decision-making. Dinner rotates between your two Sunday-prepped proteins so it's not the exact same plate seven nights running.
    Ground Rules

    Eat freely: beef, pork, chicken (esp. thighs — cheaper, more fat, harder to overcook), eggs, bacon, full-fat cheese, butter, olive oil, avocado oil, heavy cream. Protein and fat are both on your side here.

    Avoid: bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, sugar, most fruit, beer. These blow the carb ceiling fast and you won't get much volume of food for the carbs spent.

    Vegetables: you don't like them and this plan doesn't lean on them. If you want fiber for regularity, a spoon of chia or psyllium husk in water does the job without needing to eat a salad.

    Electrolytes: the first 1–2 weeks off carbs, you'll lose water weight fast and can feel flat or crampy ("keto flu") from sodium/potassium/magnesium loss. Salt your food more than feels normal, and consider a sugar-free electrolyte mix (LMNT or similar) daily.

    Alcohol: track it in your check-ins. Most beer is carb-heavy; straight liquor (whiskey, tequila, vodka) is effectively zero-carb but still counts against fat loss and messes with sleep/recovery.

    Weekly Meal Prep — Sunday, ~90 Minutes
    StepWhatTime
    1Oven to 375°F. Bake a tray of 8–10 chicken thighs (skin on, salted heavy) — covers dinners.~40 min (mostly hands-off)
    2While that's in, brown 3–4 lb ground beef in batches, season heavy (salt, pepper, garlic powder, cumin/chili if you want taco-style). Covers lunches.~25 min
    3Bake a dozen eggs muffin-tin style with cooked bacon/sausage crumbles and shredded cheese, 350°F. Covers breakfasts.~25 min (overlap with step 1)
    4Hard boil a dozen eggs for grab-and-go snacks.~15 min
    5Portion everything into containers: 2 egg muffins + breakfast, beef + cheese for lunch, chicken thigh + butter for dinner.~10 min
    Rotate the protein weekly if you want variety (swap ground beef for pork shoulder, chicken thighs for steak tips) — the structure stays the same, just the protein source changes.

    Why This Works For You Specifically

    Zero reliance on vegetables — every meal is meat, egg, cheese, and fat. Everything here batch-cooks in one Sunday session and reheats without going bad by Thursday. High protein target protects muscle while the calorie deficit does the work on the scale, and low carb should keep your already-strong glucose numbers (87.6% time-in-range) exactly where they are or better.

    The Program — 26 Weeks to Match the 6-Month Goal

    Phase 1 — Wks 1–4

    Technique & Base

    Lighter bells, higher reps, groove the patterns. Mostly 35/55 lb. Calisthenics: fewer rounds, focus on form.

    Phase 2 — Wks 5–8

    Strength Build

    Heavier bells (55/70 lb), lower reps on mains. Calisthenics: add a round.

    Phase 3 — Wks 9–12

    Conditioning

    Complexes, shorter rest, full use of the 70 lb bell. Calisthenics: shorter rest between rounds.

    Phase 4 — Wks 13–26

    Sustain & Push Volume

    Repeat Phase 3 loads. Every 4 weeks: add a round to calisthenics, add 5 reps to carries, or add 5–10 min to a cardio day. This is where the deficit needs training volume to back it up.

    Tai Chi — Lymphatic Drainage & Recovery

    The point of tai chi here isn't strength — it's slow, rhythmic, low-impact movement paired with deep breathing, which helps move lymphatic fluid (your lymph system has no pump of its own; it relies on muscle contraction and movement) and shifts you out of a training-stressed state before the rest of the day starts.

    WhenLengthStructure
    Before every 4–5am session~10 minSlow weight shifts side to side, gentle arm circles/"cloud hands," a few minutes of deep belly breathing. This is your warm-up, not a separate workout.
    Sunday~20 minA basic 8–form sequence (e.g. Commencement, Part the Wild Horse's Mane, White Crane Spreads Wings, Brush Knee, Repulse Monkey) done slowly, 2–3 rounds through.
    Tai chi form is genuinely hard to learn from text — a short beginner video series or a local class will get you correct form far faster than a written description. Treat the structure above as the schedule/dosage; get the actual movements from a visual source. It's naturally knee-friendly (weight stays controlled and centered), but keep stances shallow while the knee is a concern.
