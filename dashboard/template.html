    18 hours fasted, 6-hour eating window (~1–6 PM covers it with room to spare). Water, black coffee, and plain tea don't break the fast. Electrolytes matter more here than on a normal eating schedule — don't skip the salt.
    This Week's Meals
    Meal 1 is batch-cooked once and repeats all week — less decision-making during the harder-to-plan midday window. Meal 2 rotates through five different proteins across the week instead of just alternating two, so dinner doesn't get monotonous. The "Meal plan followed" line on each card reflects a meal_followed column in your check-in Sheet — log Y/N there daily to see it update here and feed the 30-Day Health Score.
    Ground Rules

    Eat freely: beef, pork, chicken, eggs, bacon, full-fat cheese, butter, olive oil, avocado oil, heavy cream. Protein and fat are both on your side here. Default to leaner cuts (skinless thighs, 90/10 beef) as your everyday base — see "Why These Quantities" below for why — then add butter/oil/cheese back in freely to taste and hit your calorie target.

    Avoid: bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, sugar, most fruit, beer. These blow the carb ceiling fast and you won't get much volume of food for the carbs spent.

    Vegetables: you don't like them and this plan doesn't lean on them. If you want fiber for regularity, a spoon of chia or psyllium husk in water does the job without needing to eat a salad.

    Electrolytes: the first 1–2 weeks off carbs, you'll lose water weight fast and can feel flat or crampy ("keto flu") from sodium/potassium/magnesium loss. Salt your food more than feels normal, and consider a sugar-free electrolyte mix (LMNT or similar) daily.

    Alcohol: track it in your check-ins. Most beer is carb-heavy; straight liquor (whiskey, tequila, vodka) is effectively zero-carb but still counts against fat loss and messes with sleep/recovery.

    Weekly Meal Prep — Sunday, ~90 Minutes
    StepWhatTime
    1Oven to 375°F. Brown 3–4 lb 90/10 ground beef in batches, season heavy (salt, pepper, garlic powder, cumin/chili if you want taco-style). Covers Meal 1 all week + Tuesday/Thursday dinners.~25 min
    2Bake a dozen eggs muffin-tin style with cooked bacon crumbles and shredded cheese, 350°F. Covers Meal 1 all week.~25 min
    3Bake 3–4 boneless, skinless chicken thighs (~2 lb raw, salted heavy) — covers Monday/Wednesday/Friday dinners.~35 min (mostly hands-off)
    4Portion Meal 1 (eggs + beef) and the chicken dinners into containers.~10 min
    Salmon (Thursday) and steak (Friday, if you swap it in for chicken) hold up better cooked fresh same-day rather than reheated from Sunday — buy those two separately and cook them right before Meal 2 on the day. Everything else batch-cooks fine.

    Quantities are cooked weight. A kitchen scale is the easiest way to hit these consistently — weigh after cooking, not raw. Meal 1 is a big single sitting (~121g protein, ~1,210 kcal) — take your time with it, hot sauce/mustard helps with an all-meat plate.

    Why These Quantities

    240g of protein a day is a lot of food — roughly 2 lb of meat/eggs/cheese combined, now split across just two sittings instead of four. Hitting that on the fattier "eat freely" cuts (skin-on thighs, 80/20 beef) overshoots your fat and calorie target by 300–400 kcal/day, even though it undershoots protein. Leaner cuts (skinless thigh, 90/10 beef, pork tenderloin) get you closer to all three numbers at once — roughly 230g protein, 115–135g fat, ~2,000–2,150 kcal, under 10g carb on a typical day — with calorie room left over to add butter or oil back in to taste, especially on the leaner Meal 2 nights (pork, chicken).

    Don't chase the numbers to the gram every day. Use your check-in log and the scale trend as the real feedback loop, and treat this table as the starting point, not a rulebook.

    The Program — 26 Weeks to Match the 6-Month Goal

    Phase 1 — Wks 1–4

    Technique & Base

    Lighter bells, higher reps, groove the patterns. Mostly 35/55 lb. Calisthenics: fewer rounds, focus on form.

    Phase 2 — Wks 5–8

    Strength Build

    Heavier bells (55/70 lb), lower reps on mains. Calisthenics: add a round.

    Phase 3 — Wks 9–12

    Conditioning

    Complexes, shorter rest, full use of the 70 lb bell. Calisthenics: shorter rest between rounds.

    Phase 4 — Wks 13–26

    Sustain & Push Volume

    Repeat Phase 3 loads. Every 4 weeks: add a round to calisthenics, add 5 reps to carries, or add 5–10 min to a cardio day. This is where the deficit needs training volume to back it up.

    Tai Chi — Lymphatic Drainage & Recovery

    The point of tai chi here isn't strength — it's slow, rhythmic, low-impact movement paired with deep breathing, which helps move lymphatic fluid (your lymph system has no pump of its own; it relies on muscle contraction and movement) and shifts you out of a training-stressed state before the rest of the day starts.

    WhenLengthStructure
    Before every 4–5am session~10 minSlow weight shifts side to side, gentle arm circles/"cloud hands," a few minutes of deep belly breathing. This is your warm-up, not a separate workout.
    Sunday~20 minA basic 8–form sequence (e.g. Commencement, Part the Wild Horse's Mane, White Crane Spreads Wings, Brush Knee, Repulse Monkey) done slowly, 2–3 rounds through.
    Tai chi form is genuinely hard to learn from text — a short beginner video series or a local class will get you correct form far faster than a written description. Treat the structure above as the schedule/dosage; get the actual movements from a visual source. It's naturally knee-friendly (weight stays controlled and centered), but keep stances shallow while the knee is a concern.
